Getting Started

feets puts every run, walk, hike and ride you've recorded on one map. You can see where you've been, how much of your neighbourhood you've covered, and plan routes through the streets you haven't been down yet.

Getting your activities in

  • Upload files: open Add tracks and drop GPX, FIT or TCX files on the box, or click it to choose them. FIT files are what Garmin, Coros, Wahoo and Suunto watches record, so you can copy them straight off the watch. Gzipped files (.fit.gz, .gpx.gz) work too: the activities folder in Strava's "Download your data" archive can go in all at once.
  • Import from Strava: in Add tracks, connect your Strava account, then use Import from Strava to bring in your activities. Refresh names picks up names and activity types you've changed on Strava since.

Pace, moving time, best efforts and climb are worked out for each track after it arrives. For a big import, this can take a little while.

The map modes

The buttons along the top of the map switch what it shows:

  • Tracks: every activity as its own coloured line. Click a line for its distance, pace, best efforts and elevation chart. Race a past run there finds your other activities on the same route, and plays two back together against the clock, showing who's ahead and by how much.
  • Heatmap: all your activities as one glowing layer on a dark map. The places you go most shine brightest.
  • Questing: the game side of feets. Your level, this week's quests and your trophies are in a column on the right of the map (on a phone, in the panel under it), and the map is hidden under fog of war: a dark fog, cleared in a band along everywhere you've been on foot, so the places you've never been are the dark bits. Clearing sets how wide the band is: 250 m to start with, which reveals the blocks either side of a street you've been down. The filters count: pick a year to see just that year's clearings. The Fog slider sets how dark it is. Fog grows back makes it a game of keeping your ground: a place stays clear for a while after you've been, then the fog slowly returns until it's dark again, unless you go back. Within a month: clear for a week. Within 3 months: clear for a month. Within 6 months: clear for 2 months. Within a year: clear for 6 months.
  • Road Fill: pick an area, such as your neighbourhood or city, and see which of its streets you've covered, coloured by how much of each one. It also shows your coverage overall and by neighbourhood, and a Still to cover list. Tick neighbourhoods under By neighbourhood to count only those, or pick several areas… in the area menu to colour in a few areas at once (without the totals). Click a street you can't use, like a highway, and choose Mark off-limits so it stops counting. feets also adds street maps by itself, for neighbourhoods you've been to a few times. It checks twice a day at most, while feets is open. Turn this off under Account with Add street maps where I've been.
  • Planning: draw a new route along the streets of your areas. See Planning a route below.

Fade map softens the background map so your lines stand out (not in Planning or Questing). The layers button at the top right of the map switches between the street map, a topographic map and a dark map. Each mode remembers the one you last picked in it: the heatmap starts on the dark map, the others on the street map.

Your track list

  • Search and filter by name, year or activity type. Stats and the map follow the filters.
  • Tick a track to show or hide it on the map, or use Show all and Hide all.
  • ◎ shows just that one track on the map. Click it again to bring the others back.
  • Track colours: every track gets its own colour, or pick a single colour for all of them.
  • Edit shows more buttons on each track:
    • ⤳ plans a new route from that activity.
    • ✎ removes stray GPS points: click the bad points, or Shift-drag a box around them, then Save.
    • ↓ downloads the original GPX.
    • × deletes the track.

Stats, goals and trophies

  • This week (in Questing): your streak, the weeks in a row you've covered at least 100 m of new street, and three quests made from your own gaps: new streets in a nearby neighbourhood, a street you've nearly finished, and new streets anywhere. They're ticked off as your activities arrive, and there are new ones every Monday. Plan it sets up Planning for a quest.
  • Segments: short stretches you time yourself on, like a favourite hill or a fast straight. Click an activity's line on the map, choose Make a segment, click where it starts and where it ends, and name it. feets finds every time you've done it, on foot, and keeps a leaderboard of your times, with a 🏅 note when you set a new best.
  • Stats: totals and a calendar of your activity. Pick a year in the filters for a time frame.
  • Goals: set kilometres per week and per month, and watch your progress.
  • Trophies (in Questing): milestones you earn along the way, all time and for each year, including streaks of 4, 8, 12, 26 and 52 weeks with new streets. Past activities count too.
  • Level (in Questing): experience points (XP) for what you do from now on. Only you see them.

Levels and XP

XP only counts from the day it started for your account (shown in the Level box), so adding years of old activities doesn't level you up. An activity earns XP if it was recorded since then and added within a week of being recorded, as Strava or your watch would.

  • Each activity: 10 XP, and more the longer it is (a 5 km run is about 32, a 10 km run 42). Rides count a third of their distance.
  • New streets: 10 XP a km of street you'd never covered.
  • A new best over 1K, 5K, 10K, a half or a marathon: 40 XP. On a segment: 20.
  • A week that keeps your streak going: 25.
  • A quest done: 50. A trophy: 15.

Level 2 takes 200 XP, and each level after takes 50 more than the last. Deleting an activity takes away the XP it earned.

XP can slip too, a little:

  • A quest not done by the end of its week: 20.
  • A week without an activity: after 7 days with none, each day after costs a bit more than the last: 5, then 10, 15 and so on, up to 50 a day. Your next activity starts the week again.

Losses never take you below the start of your level, so you can't lose a level, only progress towards the next.

Planning a route

Planning needs at least one area from Road Fill, because routes follow its streets.

  • Click the map to add points. The route finds its way along the streets between them.
  • Drag a point to move it, or click it to remove it. Click a stretch of the line to make it a straight line instead (for a path the map doesn't know about).
  • Undo, Back to start, Reverse and New do what they say.
  • Make a loop builds a loop of the length you choose. With Through streets I haven't covered ticked, it goes for streets you've missed. Untick it for a loop over any streets. The slider sets how winding it can be. Simple makes a plain loop through a few long stretches. Thorough takes in as many different streets as fit. Heading makes the loop favour one direction from the start, such as south. Finish streets I've started goes for streets you've partly covered. No dead ends or doubling back keeps out of cul-de-sacs and off streets the route has already used, which suits leading a group. Streets can prefer paths and trails, or keep to roads. Stay in keeps the whole loop inside one of your areas or neighbourhoods. Hills keeps it flatter or goes looking for climbs. With Finish set to at my last point, click a start and then a finish on the map, then Make a loop for a route of about that length from one to the other.
  • GPS art draws a picture with your route: pick a shape (a heart, a star, a fish…) and an area, and feets finds where it fits the streets best.
  • ▶ (top right of the map) traces the route from start to finish, leaving an orange trail. It shows which way to go where a route crosses or doubles back on itself. Pause it any time; 1× changes the speed. Anyone you share the route with can use it too.
  • Save keeps the route in Saved routes. Download GPX sends it to your watch or Strava.

Every option is explained in Planning routes.

Sharing

  • Link on a saved route makes a link anyone can open without logging in. It works for 7 days.
  • Share view link under Account shares all your saved routes, for example with a club. Turn a link off any time from the same place.

More in Guests and sharing.

Finding your way around the map

  • ⌖ (under the zoom buttons) shows where you are now.
  • Home: under Account, Set home to this view makes the map open at your current spot and zoom. The ⌂ button takes you back there any time.

On a phone, the panel sits under the map. Tap Hide panel for a full-screen map.

Miles or kilometres, light or dark

  • Units: pick Kilometres or Miles under Account, next to Language (or on the login screen). Distances, paces, climbs, goals and the lengths you type in Planning all follow it. feets starts in miles if your browser is set to US English.
  • Dark mode: the ☾ button beside the title. Until you press it, feets follows your device's setting.

Putting feets on your home screen

  • iPhone and iPad: in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Android, and Chrome or Edge on a computer: use Install feets as an app at the bottom of the About feets box, or Install in the browser's menu.

It then opens in its own window, without the browser's address bar. It opens without a connection too, though your tracks need one to load.
