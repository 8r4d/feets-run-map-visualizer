Planning routes

Plan mode draws routes along the streets of your areas. You can draw one point by point, or let Make a loop build one for you, and then change it, save it, put it on your watch or share it.

Routes only follow streets in your areas. If Plan mode says there's no street nearby, add an area for that part of the map in Streets mode first. feets also adds areas by itself for places you've been a few times (see Add street maps where I've been under Account).

Drawing a route yourself

  • Click the map to add a point. The route finds the shortest way along the streets from the last point. The green dot is the start and the red one the finish.
  • Drag a point to move it. Click a point to remove it.
  • Click the line between two points for Straight line here. Use it for a path, field or crossing the map doesn't have; it's drawn dashed. Follow the streets puts it back.
  • If two points have no streets joining them, the route draws a dashed straight line between them.
  • Undo removes the last point. Back to start adds a last leg home to the first point. Reverse turns the route the other way round. New starts again, and asks first if the route isn't saved.

What the panel tells you

  • Distance, climb, and new street: for example "6.0 km · ↑ 59 m · 2.3 km on new streets".
  • The elevation chart appears a moment after you stop changing the route. Heights come from Open-Meteo's free service. If it can't be reached, or its daily limit is used up, some heights are estimated, and the chart says so.
  • Show street coverage colours the streets as in Streets mode, so you can aim for the red ones you haven't covered. Untick it for a plainer map.
  • Arrows along the line show the way round. ▶ at the top right of the map traces the route from start to finish (see below).

Make a loop

Set a length and click Make a loop. feets builds a route of about that length and draws it, ready to change like one you drew yourself.

  • Where it starts: at the route's first point, if you've placed one. Otherwise from where you are, if the ⌖ location dot is in view. Otherwise from the middle of the map. The message after it's made says which.
  • How long it comes out: the length is a most, not an exact target. Loops usually come out at 85–100% of it. In a small area, or with options that leave little to choose from, a loop can come out shorter, because a bigger one won't fit.
  • It isn't saved until you click Save. Making another loop asks before throwing away one you haven't saved.

The Options box under the button holds the settings below. When it's folded away, its heading lists any you've changed, for example "Options · heading south, flatter". Your settings are remembered in this browser.

Through streets I haven't covered

Ticked (the usual setting), the loop goes for streets you haven't covered. Unticked, it makes a loop over any streets, which is handy somewhere new or for a route for someone else.

What counts as "covered" is the tracks on the map right now. Hidden tracks, the year and type filters, and ◎ all change it. For example, pick this year in the filters to find streets you haven't covered this year, or pick one activity type so only that counts.

Streets you've marked off-limits are never aimed for. A route crosses or follows one only when there's no reasonable way round, because they count as three times longer.

Finish streets I've started

This one works with Through streets I haven't covered. It's greyed out when that's unticked. It favours streets you've already partly covered (the orange ones), counting them three times as much as untouched streets. Your count of completed streets then goes up faster, though the loop may pick up a little less new street in all.

No dead ends or doubling back (for groups)

The loop stays out of cul-de-sacs and paths that go nowhere, and avoids going back along streets it has already used. That makes it much easier to lead a group round, with no "now turn round and come back" moments.

  • A dead end means any street you can only leave the way you came in. New streets like that are skipped altogether.
  • If your start is itself on a dead-end street, the route still uses it, since there's no other way out.
  • Expect a little less new street per loop, and a second or so longer to make it.
  • It works best with the Simple end of the slider. The thorough levels can still have the odd short U-turn.

Simple ↔ Thorough

The slider sets how winding the loop can be, in five steps:

  • 1: a plain loop through one or two long stretches you haven't covered. The easiest to follow.
  • 2: up to three stretches.
  • 3 (the middle): up to four stretches.
  • 4: goes after many shorter stretches, a few side trips per km, and takes in more new streets on the way between them.
  • 5: as many new streets as fit, however much it winds. The most new street, and the hardest to follow without a watch.

Levels 1 to 3 try every combination of the longest stretches in reach and keep the best loop, so they rarely retrace their steps. Levels 4 and 5 build the loop a stretch at a time, picking up whatever is close.

Heading

Makes the loop favour one direction from the start, such as south. It only aims for new streets roughly that way (within about 50° of it). Streets right by the start are always allowed, since a loop has to leave and come back. If there isn't enough that way, it looks wider (about 80°), and failing that ignores the heading. The message then says it went wider.

Streets

  • Any kind: roads, paths and trails as they come.
  • Prefer paths and trails: aims for paths and trails, and counts roads as longer to get about on. Roads still link things up where there's no path.
  • Roads only: doesn't aim for paths or trails, and only uses one when going round would be about four times longer. Good for a rainy day, or when a buggy or wheelchair is coming along.

Whether a way counts as a road, path or trail comes from OpenStreetMap. Footways and cycleways are paths. Tracks, mountain-bike paths, and paths with a dirt, grass, sand or woodchip surface are trails. Sidewalks aren't separate paths; they count as part of their street.

Hills

  • Flatter keeps off steep streets: a street rising 1 m in 25 counts as twice as long.
  • Hillier goes looking for them: the same street counts as half as long.

To do this, feets looks up the heights of the ground around the start (and the finish) before it plans, on a grid about every 300 m. That's a few requests to Open-Meteo, the same free service the elevation chart uses, and the heights are kept in this browser. If the heights can't be had (offline, the service busy, or its daily limit used up), the loop is made anyway and the message says hills weren't taken into account.

The grid is coarse, so it knows about real hills but not a short steep ramp. Check the elevation chart once the loop is made.

Finish

  • Back at the start: a loop.
  • At my last point: a route from one place to another. Click the map for a start, then a finish, then Make a loop. You get a route of about the chosen length from the first point to the last. It works for getting to work, or ending at a café or a friend's place.

The length has to be more than the shortest way between the two points by the streets, or no route fits. Your two points are replaced by the route, without asking, since they were only there to say where to go.

Stay in

Keeps the whole route inside one of your areas. Pick the area, then open All its neighbourhoods under it and tick one or more of them to stay inside just those. It's good for finishing off a neighbourhood or two.

  • Neighbourhoods that don't touch each other can't be joined up in one loop. The loop stays inside whichever one it starts in.
  • If your start (or finish) is outside, the route starts from the nearest street inside instead, and the message says so.
  • Streets along the edge belong to whichever side they're on. A loop may not use a road that lies right along the boundary.
  • The smaller the area, the less room there is. A long loop inside a small neighbourhood will come out shorter, or doubling back.

Putting options together

Every option you add narrows what the loop can use. They all work together, but a lot of them at once, in a small space, can leave nothing that fits. Then you'll see "No new streets fit… Try a longer one, or fewer options".

  • The combinations that most often leave nothing are Stay in a small neighbourhood, a Heading, and Roads only or No dead ends, together with a short length.
  • Loosen one at a time: a longer length, any way for heading, then a bigger area.
  • If you've covered almost everything nearby, untick Through streets I haven't covered, or hide some tracks so their streets count as new again.

After it's made

  • Change it like any route. Drag a point to take it down a different street. Click a point in the middle to remove it, and the route reroutes between its neighbours. Clicking the start or finish drops that end of the route.
  • ▶ Trace (top right of the map) sends a marker round it from start to finish, leaving an orange trail, so you can see which way to go where the route crosses itself. 1× changes the speed.
  • Save keeps it under Saved routes, and Download GPX gets it onto your watch or into Strava.

GPS art

Under GPS art, pick a shape, one of your areas, and a length, then Find best fit. The Shapes menu switches between everyday shapes and seasonal ones: Halloween (pumpkin, ghost, bat, witch hat) and Christmas (tree, candy cane, stocking, snowman, gift, bell, gingerbread man, ornament). There are also bubble letters and numbers, A to Z and 0 to 9. Letters with a hole, like A, B or 8, have a short spoke into the hole and back, so each one can be run in one go. It opens on the season's: Halloween in October, Christmas from mid-November. feets tries the shape at thousands of places, sizes and angles in that area. It draws the best few along the streets and shows the one that matches the shape most closely. The shape itself stays on the map as a faint purple line, so you can see how close it came.

  • The message says how close: for example "on average 20 m from the shape (95% within 52 m)". Lower is better. Next fit steps through the other good ones, from best to worst.
  • Only the shape counts. Coverage, hills and doubling back are all ignored, and the route goes over the same street twice whenever that keeps it closer to the drawing. Streets marked off-limits are still avoided where there's a way round.
  • Moving it by hand: the shape gets two purple handles. Drag ✥ to move it, or ⟳ to turn it and make it bigger or smaller. The outline follows as you drag, with its length, and letting go draws it on the streets again. Place on map puts the shape in the middle of the map to start from, rather than searching. If much of the shape is away from the area's streets, the message says so.
  • Rotate freely lets it try any angle. Otherwise it stays within 30° of upright, and prefers upright, since shapes read best that way.
  • Size matters. Small details like a cat's ears need room: on a grid of 100 m blocks, a short shape comes out as a blob. If the match is poor, try a longer one, or a bigger area. If the shape doesn't fit the area at that length, the message says so.
  • The result is an ordinary route. Drag points to tidy it up, then save it, download the GPX, or share it. ▶ trace is a nice way to watch it being drawn.

Saved routes

  • Click a saved route to open it. Opening another asks first if the one you're on has unsaved changes.
  • Sort them by newest, name, or length.
  • GPX downloads it. Rename and Delete do what they say.
  • Link makes a link anyone can open without logging in, and you can set it to expire. Anyone with it can also see your other saved routes. See Guests and sharing.
